# Sustainable MICE Events in Namibia: How to Plan a Greener Conference

A practical guide to reducing the environmental impact of conferences, meetings and corporate events in Namibia.

Sustainability is no longer simply a corporate buzzword. For international companies, it is increasingly becoming part of how destinations, venues and event suppliers are evaluated.

For Namibia, this presents a significant opportunity.

With its strong conservation heritage, vast natural landscapes, wildlife initiatives and growing focus on responsible tourism, Namibia provides an ideal setting for companies looking to create more sustainable MICE events.

“A greener event is not about doing everything differently. It is about making better decisions at every stage of the event.”

## What Is a Sustainable MICE Event?

A sustainable MICE event considers the environmental, social and economic impact of the event — from planning and transport to catering, accommodation and waste management.

For a conference or corporate event, this could mean:

Reducing unnecessary printing

Minimising single-use plastics

Choosing local suppliers

Reducing food waste

Supporting local communities

Using locally sourced products

Managing energy and water responsibly

Choosing responsible transport options

Incorporating conservation into the programme

Sustainability does not necessarily mean compromising on quality.

It means being more intentional about how quality is delivered.

# 1. Choose the Right Venue

Your venue is one of the most important sustainability decisions.

When selecting a conference venue in Namibia, ask about:

Energy efficiency

Water management

Waste separation

Recycling

Local sourcing

Sustainable catering

Plastic reduction

Environmental policies

Community initiatives

Do not simply ask whether a venue is “green”.

Ask what it actually does.

# 2. Reduce Paper and Printed Materials

A modern MICE event can significantly reduce paper consumption.

Instead of printing large quantities of:

Programmes

Menus

Maps

Presentations

Delegate information

use:

Digital programmes

QR codes

Event apps

Digital registration

Online surveys

For materials that do need to be printed, consider recycled or responsibly sourced paper.

# 3. Reduce Single-Use Plastics

Small changes can make a significant difference across a large event.

Consider replacing:

Plastic water bottles → Refillable water stations

Disposable name badges → Reusable badge holders

Plastic conference materials → Digital alternatives

Single-use catering items → Reusable crockery and cutlery

These measures can also improve the overall appearance and professionalism of an event.

# 4. Choose Local Suppliers

Sustainability is not only about the environment.

It is also about creating positive economic impact.

Where practical, work with local:

Event companies

Transport providers

Caterers

Guides

Entertainment

Decor suppliers

Activity operators

Accommodation providers

Using local suppliers can help keep more of the event's economic value within Namibia while reducing unnecessary logistics.

“A sustainable event should leave something positive behind for the destination — not only memories for the delegates.”

# 5. Serve Local and Seasonal Food

Food is an important part of the MICE experience and a major opportunity to improve sustainability.

Consider menus that incorporate:

Locally sourced ingredients

Seasonal produce

Namibian dishes

Regional specialities

Local beverages where appropriate

Work with caterers to reduce food waste by accurately estimating delegate numbers.

Excess food can also potentially be managed through appropriate food-donation or waste-reduction partnerships, where available.

# 6. Make Transport More Efficient

Transport can have a significant environmental impact, particularly for large conferences.

Instead of arranging multiple individual transfers, consider coordinated group transportation.

For example:

Airport → Hotel → Conference Venue → Evening Function

Group transfers can reduce unnecessary vehicle movements and make logistics easier to manage.

For multi-destination programmes, carefully plan routes to avoid unnecessary backtracking.

# 7. Incorporate Conservation Into the Programme

This is where Namibia has a particularly strong opportunity.

Instead of treating sustainability as something that happens behind the scenes, make it part of the delegate experience.

Corporate groups can consider:

Conservation-focused activities

Wildlife experiences

Environmental education

Community-based tourism

Responsible nature experiences

This allows delegates to understand Namibia's conservation story while creating a more meaningful event.

“The most memorable sustainable experiences are those that allow delegates to see the positive impact for themselves.”

# 8. Support Local Communities

A sustainable MICE programme should benefit people as well as the environment.

Consider incorporating locally owned businesses and community-based experiences into your programme.

This could include:

Local cultural experiences

Community tourism

Local crafts

Small businesses

Local performers

Community guides

For international delegates, this also provides a more authentic understanding of Namibia.

# 9. Manage Waste Before, During and After the Event

Waste management should be considered during the planning stage rather than once the event begins.

Create a simple waste strategy covering:

### Before the Event

Reduce unnecessary printing and packaging.

### During the Event

Provide clearly marked waste and recycling stations where appropriate.

### After the Event

Ensure materials, equipment and unused products are handled responsibly.

The simplest way to manage waste is often to avoid creating it in the first place.

# 10. Choose Experiences Over Excessive Material Gifts

Corporate gifts are common at MICE events, but not every delegate needs another branded item.

Consider alternatives such as:

Locally produced gifts

Handmade crafts

Sustainable products

Digital gifts

Local experiences

Donations to an appropriate community or conservation initiative

If you do provide physical gifts, choose items that are useful, locally sourced and responsibly produced.

# 11. Communicate Your Sustainability Efforts

If sustainability is part of the event, tell delegates what you are doing.

For example:

“This event has reduced printed materials by using a digital programme.”

Or:

“Your conference meal features locally sourced ingredients from Namibian suppliers.”

Simple communication helps delegates understand that sustainability is an intentional part of the event.

However, avoid making environmental claims that cannot be verified.

Good sustainability communication should be transparent, specific and evidence-based.

# 12. Measure the Impact

For larger corporate events, consider measuring key sustainability indicators.

Depending on the event, you could track:

Number of printed materials avoided

Amount of waste generated

Percentage of local suppliers used

Food waste

Number of reusable items used

Group transport utilisation

Local procurement

Delegate participation in sustainability activities

This creates useful information for internal ESG reporting and future event planning.

# A Simple Sustainable MICE Checklist

Before your event, ask:

☑ Have we selected a venue with responsible environmental practices?

☑ Can we reduce printed materials?

☑ Can we eliminate unnecessary single-use plastics?

☑ Are we using local suppliers?

☑ Are we incorporating local food?

☑ Have we planned group transportation efficiently?

☑ Can we include a conservation or community experience?

☑ Do we have a waste-management plan?

☑ Are our corporate gifts sustainable and useful?

☑ Can we measure the results?

If the answer to most of these questions is yes, you are already moving towards a more responsible event.

# Why Namibia Is a Strong Destination for Sustainable MICE

Namibia's greatest sustainability advantage is its connection between tourism, conservation and nature.

For international corporate groups, this creates an opportunity to combine business objectives with meaningful destination experiences.

A conference can be followed by a conservation experience.

A leadership retreat can incorporate responsible tourism.

An incentive programme can support local communities.

A corporate dinner can showcase locally sourced cuisine.

The result is an event that creates value for the company, the delegates and the destination.

“Sustainability should not be an additional feature of your MICE event. It should be part of how the experience is designed.”

# Final Thoughts

A sustainable MICE event does not need to be complicated.

It starts with small, deliberate decisions:

Choose responsibly.
 Source locally.
 Reduce waste.
 Use resources wisely.
 Support communities.
 Protect the destination.

Namibia gives corporate planners a unique opportunity to combine business, conservation and unforgettable experiences in one destination.

And when done properly, a greener event is not only better for the environment.

It is better for the people, better for the destination and often better for the delegate experience.
