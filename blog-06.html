Namibia MICE Visa: What International Delegates Need to Know

Travel10 min readZusammen Travels MICE

A practical guide to Namibia's MICE visa facilitation process for international conference, meeting, incentive and exhibition delegates.

Planning an international conference, incentive programme, meeting or exhibition in Namibia involves much more than selecting a venue and arranging accommodation.

For international organisers, visa and immigration requirements are an important part of the planning process.

The good news is that Namibia has introduced a dedicated MICE e-Visa facilitation process designed to help qualifying international delegates attending MICE events in the country.

According to the Namibia Convention Bureau (NCB), the process applies to MICE-related events with more than 10 international delegates and involves coordination between the event organiser, the NCB and Namibia's Ministry of Home Affairs, Immigration, Safety and Security (MHAISS).

For international event planners, visa facilitation is not simply an administrative detail. It is an important part of creating a seamless delegate experience from registration to arrival.

This guide explains how the process works, what organisers need to prepare and what international delegates should know before travelling to Namibia.

What Is the Namibia MICE Visa?

The Namibia MICE Visa is a dedicated visa facilitation mechanism for qualifying international delegates attending Meetings, Incentives, Conferences and Exhibitions (MICE) in Namibia.

Rather than every delegate navigating the process independently from the beginning, the system allows the event organiser or convener to initiate the process through the Namibia Convention Bureau.

The NCB then coordinates the relevant steps with MHAISS.

The process is designed to provide a more structured approach to visa applications for international MICE events.

Who Can Use the MICE Visa Process?

The NCB states that the MICE e-Visa process is designed for MICE-related events with more than 10 international delegates.

This can be relevant to international:

  • Conference delegates
  • Meeting participants
  • Incentive travellers
  • Presenters
  • Facilitators
  • Exhibitors
  • Resource persons
  • Event organisers
  • International media attending qualifying events

However, visa requirements can vary according to nationality and the purpose of travel.

Some nationalities may not require a visa for their particular category of entry, while others may need to apply.

Always verify the latest requirements before travelling.

How Does the Namibia MICE Visa Process Work?

One of the most important things international planners should understand is that the process begins before individual delegates submit their applications.

The NCB outlines a six-step process.

Step 1: Notify the Namibia Convention Bureau

The event organiser or convener first notifies the NCB about the upcoming event.

The NCB currently advises organisers to notify it at least two months before the event to allow sufficient time for the required processes.

The notification should include information such as:

  • Event description
  • Event dates
  • Event venue
  • Expected number of delegates
  • Number of international participants
  • Request for MICE visa facilitation
  • Organiser's background and experience
  • Previous MICE events
  • Relevant affiliations
  • Responsible contact person's details

The request must also be accompanied by an endorsement letter from a Government Office, Ministry or Agency (OMA), or a State-Owned Enterprise (SOE) supporting the event.

Important planning tip

Do not leave this until the last few weeks before your event.

Visa planning should begin at the same time as venue and accommodation planning.

Step 2: Submit the International Delegate List

Once the event has been initiated, the organiser must provide the international delegate list using the prescribed NCB template.

The information required includes:

  • First name
  • Surname
  • Date of birth
  • Passport number
  • Nationality
  • Purpose of visit

The NCB specifies permitted purposes such as:

  • Participant
  • Presenter
  • Facilitator
  • Exhibitor
  • Media/Journalist

The organiser should ensure that the information is accurate and complete.

A delegate list is not simply an administrative spreadsheet. It becomes an important part of the visa facilitation process, so accuracy matters.

The NCB specifically instructs organisers not to alter the format of the template and to complete all required fields.

Step 3: Receive the MICE Event Code

Once the delegate information has been submitted and processed, the event organiser receives a unique Event Code.

This code is then shared with the delegates included on the submitted list.

The NCB states that delegates who are not included on the submitted list will not be able to apply through the MICE visa process.

This makes delegate management extremely important.

If someone joins the event at the last minute, organisers should not simply assume that the person can use the same process.

The organiser should contact the relevant authorities/NCB for guidance on how to handle additions or changes.

Step 4: Delegates Apply Online

Once they receive the Event Code, eligible delegates can proceed with their online MICE visa application.

Applicants are required to upload:

  • A passport photograph
  • A copy of their passport

The NCB currently specifies a maximum file size of 300 KB for the passport photograph and 2 MB for the passport copy in PDF format.

This is an important practical detail.

International delegates should prepare these documents before starting the application.

How Much Does the MICE Visa Cost?

The NCB currently lists the MICE Visa fee as:

N$1,600 per delegate

The fee is paid electronically once the application has been approved.

Because visa fees and immigration requirements can change, organisers should always confirm the current fee before publishing final delegate information.

How Long Does the MICE Visa Take?

According to the NCB's current MICE Visa guidance:

  • General participants: approximately 1–3 working days
  • Delegates attending in a work capacity: approximately 1–5 working days

The latter category can include presenters, facilitators, exhibitors and media.

However, delegates should not plan their travel around the minimum processing time.

Allowing additional time is always advisable in case an application requires clarification, additional information or correction.

Fast processing does not mean last-minute planning. The safest approach is to submit early and allow a buffer before travel.

What Documents Should Delegates Prepare?

Before beginning the online application, international delegates should have the required documentation ready.

For the MICE visa process, this includes:

Passport

Ensure that the passport information entered in the application matches the passport exactly.

Passport Copy

The NCB currently requires the passport copy in PDF format and specifies a maximum file size of 2 MB.

Passport Photograph

A passport photograph is required, with the NCB specifying a maximum file size of 300 KB.

Event Code

Delegates must use the Event Code provided through the event organiser.

Delegate Information

Ensure that the information submitted to the organiser is accurate.

What About International Media?

Media and journalists attending qualifying MICE events have additional requirements.

The NCB states that foreign media must obtain an accreditation letter from Namibia's Ministry of Information and Communication Technology (MICT).

The organiser must submit the relevant media list and supporting documents, including:

  • Media/journalist identification
  • Passport copy
  • Recommendation letter from the media house or convener

This should be addressed well in advance because media accreditation involves an additional process.

What Happens After the Application Is Approved?

Once the visa has been issued, the applicant receives the visa via email, according to the NCB's published process.

Delegates should:

Check that their personal information is correct.

Keep a copy of the issued visa.

Keep their passport available for travel.

Follow all instructions provided with the visa.

Carry relevant supporting travel documentation.

It is also sensible for the event organiser to keep an internal record of delegate visa status.

Does Every International Delegate Need a MICE Visa?

No.

This is an important distinction.

Visa requirements depend on the delegate's nationality, passport and purpose of travel.

The NCB's MICE system automatically filters out applicants from countries where a visa is not required for their particular category of entry.

Namibia's broader immigration framework also contains different visa and entry arrangements depending on nationality and circumstances.

The Ministry of Home Affairs, Immigration, Safety and Security publishes current visa information and requirements.

Therefore:

Do not assume that every international delegate needs to apply for the MICE Visa.

Check the current requirements for each nationality.

What International Event Planners Should Do

If you are organising a conference or corporate event in Namibia with international participants, build visa planning into your event timeline.

A practical approach is:

1. Confirm your international delegate numbers

Establish how many international participants you expect.

2. Identify delegate nationalities

Visa requirements can differ by nationality.

3. Contact the Namibia Convention Bureau early

For qualifying MICE events, begin the facilitation process well ahead of the event.

4. Collect passport information carefully

Ensure names, dates of birth, passport numbers and nationalities are accurate.

5. Prepare the delegate list

Use the prescribed format.

6. Allow sufficient processing time

Do not rely on the shortest published turnaround.

7. Communicate clearly with delegates

Give delegates instructions on:

  • When to apply
  • What documents to prepare
  • How to use the Event Code
  • What the visa costs
  • What happens after approval

8. Monitor applications

Keep track of which delegates have completed their applications and received their visas.

A Suggested MICE Visa Timeline

For international planners, this is a useful planning framework.

3–6 Months Before the Event

  • Confirm international delegate numbers
  • Identify nationalities
  • Review visa requirements
  • Contact the NCB
  • Begin preparing event documentation

At Least 2 Months Before

  • Submit the event notification to the NCB
  • Provide the required supporting documentation
  • Obtain the relevant endorsement
  • Prepare the international delegate list

The NCB specifically recommends notifying it at least two months before the event.

After Delegate List Submission

  • Receive the Event Code
  • Distribute the code securely to registered delegates
  • Provide application instructions

Before Travel

  • Confirm that delegates have received their visas where required
  • Check passport validity and travel documentation
  • Finalise airport transfers
  • Provide arrival information

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Waiting Until the Last Minute

Even if processing times can be relatively short, last-minute applications create unnecessary risk.

Submitting Incorrect Passport Details

A single incorrect character can create complications.

Adding Delegates Without Updating the Process

The MICE visa process is based on the submitted delegate list.

Ignoring Nationality-Specific Requirements

Do not assume that all delegates follow exactly the same visa process.

Forgetting Media Accreditation

International journalists may have additional requirements.

Giving Delegates Outdated Information

Visa fees, procedures and immigration requirements can change.

Always verify current requirements before sending final travel instructions.

The Role of the Namibia Convention Bureau

The Namibia Convention Bureau plays an important role in supporting international MICE events.

Its services include MICE visa facilitation, event-planning support, connections with local service providers, venue advice and assistance with logistics and site visits.

For international organisers unfamiliar with Namibia, this can be particularly valuable.

The NCB can help bridge the gap between the international event organiser and local suppliers and authorities.

Why the MICE Visa Process Matters

For international event planners, visa facilitation is more than an immigration procedure.

It can influence the overall delegate experience.

A well-managed process provides:

Clarity, Delegates know what they need to do.

Coordination, The organiser has a central process to manage.

Efficiency, Applications are handled through a dedicated MICE mechanism.

Confidence, International planners can better prepare their delegates.

Professionalism, Visa arrangements become part of the wider event-management strategy.

This is especially important when an event involves dozens or hundreds of international delegates.

Namibia Is Making International MICE Events Easier

The introduction of the dedicated MICE e-Visa process is another development supporting Namibia's ambitions as a competitive business-events destination.

The country already offers a combination of conference facilities, accommodation, professional event suppliers and distinctive incentive experiences.

The visa facilitation process adds another important component:

making it easier for international delegates to participate in events hosted in Namibia.

For planners, that means the conversation is no longer simply:

Can we host our event in Namibia?

It can increasingly become:

How can we design the best possible event experience in Namibia?

Final Checklist for International Delegates

Before travelling to Namibia for a MICE event, make sure you have:

☑ Confirmed whether you require a visa ☑ Provided accurate passport information to the organiser ☑ Been included on the official delegate list, where applicable ☑ Received the MICE Event Code, where applicable ☑ Completed your online application ☑ Uploaded the required documents ☑ Paid the applicable visa fee, where required ☑ Received your visa approval ☑ Checked your travel dates ☑ Confirmed accommodation ☑ Confirmed airport transfers ☑ Kept copies of your important travel documents

Final Thoughts

For international planners, the success of a MICE event begins long before delegates arrive in Namibia.

Visa preparation is one of the first practical steps in creating a smooth delegate journey.

By starting early, collecting accurate information and working with the appropriate Namibian authorities and event partners, organisers can significantly reduce uncertainty and make the arrival experience more seamless.

And once delegates arrive?

That is when Namibia can truly take centre stage.

From Windhoek's business facilities to the Atlantic coast, from desert landscapes to wildlife experiences, Namibia offers an extraordinary setting for international meetings, incentives, conferences and exhibitions.

A successful MICE experience begins before the delegate boards the aircraft. It begins with making the journey to Namibia as clear and seamless as possible.

Planning a MICE Event in Namibia?

If you are planning an international conference, meeting, incentive programme or exhibition in Namibia, start your visa planning early.

The Namibia Convention Bureau provides MICE visa facilitation and support for qualifying events, alongside assistance with venues, local suppliers and event logistics.

  • Namibia Convention Bureau, MICE Visa Information
  • Namibia Ministry of Home Affairs, Visa Information

Important: Immigration rules, fees, processing times and eligibility can change. International delegates and organisers should always verify the latest requirements with the relevant Namibian authorities before making travel arrangements.
